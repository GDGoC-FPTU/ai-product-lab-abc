# Phase 6: AI Interaction Log & Reflections

**Author:** Nguyen Nam Anh

### 1. How AI Assisted Me
In my capacity acting as a Business Analyst, I used generative AI primarily as a thought-partner to structure the URD (User Requirements Document) and define business logic for the transshipment and dispatching tool. An intense debate regarding the system boundaries and use cases occurred directly between the AI and me during my self-analyzing progress, rather than with anyone in the office. This allowed me to rigorously test the logic before finalizing the document. Furthermore, I deliberately avoided using Figma or heavy IDEs during this conceptual phase, relying entirely on AI text generation and markdown to structure the initial requirements.

### 2. Where AI Failed (Hallucinations/Missteps)
While the AI was strong at text generation, it struggled with structural logic and diagram formatting. When I asked it to generate representations of UML or system flow diagrams, it repeatedly hallucinated formatting elements, such as adding an unnecessary extra outer rectangular border that cluttered the core layout. Additionally, during a logic matching exercise for the system's operational rules, the AI made direct mistakes by incorrectly mapping two logic pairs, providing an output where two of the answers needed to be explicitly swapped with each other to be correct.

### 3. How I Corrected It
I had to provide direct, firm feedback to correct these structural errors. I explicitly pointed out the visual hallucination to force the removal of the extra outer border. I also had to refine my prompt constraints to stop the AI from simply echoing my own thoughts back to me, explicitly instructing it that I was looking for *its* proposed analytical answers rather than a repetition of my own. By tightening the system boundaries and enforcing strict output formats, the AI became a much more reliable tool for drafting the product scope.
